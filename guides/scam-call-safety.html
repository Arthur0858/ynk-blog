Family pause rule

Give your parent one sentence to remember

Call blocking can reduce noise, but pressure is usually the real problem. Agree on one family rule your parent can use when a call or text feels urgent:

If it asks for money, passwords, codes, account access, or a link, pause and ask family first. Do not click. Do not reply. Do not call back from the message. Use the official app, official website, or a known family contact separately.

Practice with one fake bank text or delivery message so the rule feels familiar before a real suspicious message arrives.

No tool can stop every scam or verify every caller. A useful setup combines phone settings, carrier tools, contact habits, and a family rule for suspicious messages.

Family setup checklist

Build the safe list.

Add doctors, pharmacy, bank, close family, neighbors, and care contacts before enabling aggressive filters.

Create one rule.

Use a plain rule such as: if money, password, code, or urgent pressure is mentioned, hang up and call family first.

Clean message habits.

Teach opening known apps directly instead of tapping links from unexpected texts.

Review monthly.

Check blocked calls, missed calls, and confusing prompts together so settings do not become a new problem.
