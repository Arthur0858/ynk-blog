Editorial method

How each family checklist is prepared

The structure is consistent so it is easy to follow. The instructions, failure cases and sources must remain specific to the problem.

1. Start with a family task

Each guide begins with a task an older adult should be able to understand and repeat: make a call, recover an account, verify a message or use a device without unnecessary friction.

2. Use primary sources

Operational claims are checked against official product support, U.S. government consumer guidance or an official service status page. Media reports and social posts are not used as the final instruction source.

3. Draft, then review

Automation and assisted drafting may organize a page or identify repeated structure. The editorial desk reviews the final title, actions, stop conditions, privacy boundary and official links. Assisted text is not treated as a source.

4. Keep claims conservative

The site describes setup friction, handoff steps and verification routes. It does not promise that a device prevents emergencies, that a message is fraudulent without evidence, or that a service is operating normally when an official source cannot be verified.

5. Record meaningful changes

Every weekly guide includes a review date and update reason. Corrections that change a safety action, source or product conclusion are documented and rechecked before publication.
